# Code of conduct

This project follows the behavioural expectations of the
[Contributor Covenant, version 2.1](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).
The short version is below; the linked document is the reference.

## Our pledge

We want participating in Rilldown — issues, pull requests, discussions, and
reviews — to be free of harassment for everyone, regardless of experience,
identity, background, or the platform they use.

## Expected behaviour

- Be direct about code and kind about people. Critique the change, not the
  person who wrote it.
- Assume the other person is acting in good faith until there is evidence
  otherwise.
- Keep reports reproducible: version, platform, bundle, steps, and what you
  expected instead.
- Respect the clean-room policy in [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). Do not
  post decompiled or copied code, assets, or strings from other download
  managers.
- Accept that a maintainer may decline a change that does not fit the project's
  scope — 0.1.0 targets Windows and Linux only, and the “Not in 0.1.0” list in
  the README is deliberate.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, intimidation, or sustained disruption of discussions.
- Discriminatory comments about a person's identity or background.
- Sexual attention or imagery in project spaces.
- Publishing someone's private information without permission.
- Using project spaces to distribute malware, cracked software, or instructions
  for bypassing licensing or DRM.

## Scope

This applies in the repository, its issues, pull requests, releases, and
discussions, and when someone is representing the project in public.

## Enforcement

The repository maintainers handle enforcement through GitHub: comments may be
removed, threads locked, and contributors warned, temporarily blocked from the
repository's interaction limits, or reported to GitHub for platform-level
action. GitHub's own
[report abuse](https://docs.github.com/communities/maintaining-your-safety-on-github/reporting-abuse-or-spam)
flow is available for anything that also breaks the platform rules.

There is no separate private conduct address. For a security vulnerability, use
[SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead of a public issue.
